From the hallway, a furnace that will not heat looks the same no matter what broke. Inside the cabinet, though, everything happens in a strict order: inducer on, pressure switch closed, igniter glowing, gas valve open, burners lit, flame proven, and finally the blower moving heat through the ducts. Most of the diagnosis comes down to finding the broken link. In Sanford homes, it tends to be the same handful of culprits: a flame sensor coated in oxide, a hot surface igniter cracked by thermal cycling, a pressure switch held open by a tired inducer or a plugged condensate trap, or a control board with a burned relay.

Our experienced technicians diagnose a furnace the way it operates, in order, measuring each stage as it happens. That means igniter resistance, microamp flame signal, pressure switch operation under actual inducer draft, gas valve voltage, and blower and capacitor condition. Safety checks are never optional on a gas appliance: heat exchanger inspection, leak testing on the gas connections, and flue draft verification, since a hazard is not visible at the thermostat. We finish by checking temperature rise against the data plate, making sure the burner and blower are matched the way the manufacturer designed them to.

Sanford HVAC services natural gas, propane, and electric furnaces from all major brands, from standard 80 percent units to condensing high-efficiency models with their traps, drain lines, and dual pressure switches. Our trucks carry the parts that actually fail, such as igniters, flame sensors, pressure switches, capacitors, inducer assemblies, and blower motors, so a typical no-heat call ends with the house warming up. When to Call for Furnace Repair in Sanford

Spotting the early warning signs gives you the chance to act before the situation spirals into something far more expensive. These are the signals our technicians see most often when a problem is underway:

  • A fan that keeps running while the registers blow cool air
  • The igniter glows and clicks, yet no flame ever appears
  • Grinding or high-pitched squeals from the blower
  • Soot, rust, or moisture near the furnace cabinet or vent pipe
  • Short cycling every few minutes without reaching the setpoint
  • Burners light, run a few seconds, then shut down repeatedly
  • Yellow, wavering burner flames where they should be sharp blue
  • Booming or banging at light-off from delayed ignition

The Benefits of Professional Furnace Repair

Gas, open flame, high voltage, and a flue share one cabinet in a furnace, and the most dangerous failure is the one you cannot see: a damaged heat exchanger that lets combustion gases into the air the blower sends through your home. Every morning that delayed ignition keeps booming at startup, that exchanger takes more stress. The diagnostic side has its own pitfalls. When a furnace trips its high limit, the switch itself may be bad, or the furnace may be protecting itself from a clogged filter or a failing blower. Swapping the switch in that second case removes a safety device and leaves the actual problem in place.

Our experienced technicians treat every symptom as a question about the whole system. Flame signal gets measured rather than assumed, so a healthy sensor is never replaced while a cracked igniter or weak gas valve skates by. Draft is verified at the pressure switch and the flue. Temperature rise is checked against the data plate, which is how airflow problems get caught before they shorten the exchanger's life. And the gas side is leak-tested with instruments, not a sniff and a shrug. The result is a furnace that has been proven safe and correct under a full cycle, and an honest report if the unit is nearing the point where replacement beats another repair.

Furnace Repair Prevention Tips

A few simple habits now, like regular filter changes and seasonal tune-ups, can add years to your system's life. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Put a carbon monoxide detector on every level and test them monthly
  • Put in a fresh filter monthly through the heating season, the cheapest protection your furnace can get
  • Make sure the furnace has clear combustion air; a packed utility room starves the burners
  • Book a fall tune-up so weak igniters and fouled sensors are caught before the first freeze
  • If you have a high-efficiency furnace, check that the condensate drain is flowing freely
  • Listen at light-off; clean ignition is quiet, and booming means the burners need service
  • Keep storage, lint, and especially chlorine-based products away from the furnace closet
